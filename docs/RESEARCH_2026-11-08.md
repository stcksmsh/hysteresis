# Hysteresis: Why the Arm Collapsed to One Gesture, and How to Rebuild It So It Dances

The arm collapsed to one gesture replayed at different speeds because of how the objective is built, not because of a bug. The training target was about 8 dimensions, z-scored causally over 30 s, so differences between songs were removed before the observer ever saw them. Decoding-style (mutual-information) objectives are known to be fully satisfied by tiny or purely temporal differences in behaviour. And every smoothness or safety cost put a price on posture variety while nothing rewarded it. The fix has three parts. First, change what has to be decoded: song- and section-level structure, scored contrastively. Second, make visible posture difference the only way to be decodable, using a Lipschitz-constrained observer and a population or song-level diversity term. Third, turn soft costs into hard bounds so they stop pricing variety. Then pre-register human match/mismatch tests that a one-shape solution cannot pass.

## TL;DR

- **Diagnosis:** Mutual-information and decoding objectives "usually prefer static skills" and "can be fully maximized even with small differences in states" (Park et al., LSD, ICLR 2022). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00914) [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00914) Your target was low-dimensional and normalized within each song. So one shape, modulated in timing, was an optimal solution, not a failure to optimize. The figures point the same way: peak hand speed was identical in every song (0.741 m/s), shoulder sd was 3°, and one motor program was used 100% of the time. The arm was pressed against its cost bounds, not expressing state.
- **Top changes, in rank order:**
  1. Replace the 8-D within-song z-scored target with a contrastive music↔motion matching objective plus slow, song-level state dimensions.
  2. Make the observer 1-Lipschitz in joint/posture space, so that decodability requires large visible differences.
  3. Add a determinant (DvD-style) diversity term across songs in each batch.
  4. Turn jerk, speed and limit penalties into hinge constraints with free interiors.
  5. Add a Switch-style load-balancing loss to motor-program selection, or remove it.
  6. Retry memory only as a fixed, non-trained kNN blend over motor parameters.
- **Proof and safety:** Pre-register a human 2-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) test of real vs swapped music (target ≥65% correct), plus between-song Jensen–Shannon (JS) distance, posture-count and shoulder-range bars. Beat alignment alone proves almost nothing. EDGE's generated dances beat real dancers on that metric, and in Hoffman & Vanunu (HRI 2013), 22/23 listeners believed an off-beat robot was on-beat. [guyhoffman](http://guyhoffman.com/publications/HoffmanHRI13.pdf) [liner](https://liner.com/review/edge-editable-dance-generation-from-music) On hardware: the 2.9 N·m figure is the 12 V STS3215; the 7.4 V version is rated at 16.5 kg·cm (≈1.6 N·m) at 6 V. Identify servo latency and gains before trusting any sim gesture.

## 1. Diagnosis of the current failure

### 1.1 What the measurements say

Read together, the measurements describe a policy sitting on the boundary of its cost set:

- **Peak hand speed** was 0.741 m/s in every song. A value identical to three decimals across songs is almost certainly the hand-speed hinge threshold (or the amplitude cap) being hit, not a musical choice. The policy pushes speed until the cost bites, then stops.
- **Shoulder sd** was 3°, against 14–17° for the hand-tuned planner. The shoulder carries the largest gravity load and the most visible mass, so moving it costs the most energy and jerk. A cost-minimizing policy leaves it still.
- **Posture count:** 7–10× fewer distinct postures, identical shape statistics across songs, and one program used 100% of the time. Nothing in the objective made a second shape pay for itself.

### 1.2 Why decoding/MI objectives collapse to one modulated shape

**The theory.** Legibility as you implemented it is a variational lower bound on the mutual information between hidden state and visible motion: the observer's decoding accuracy. The skill-discovery literature has documented exactly this pathology:

- **Diversity is All You Need (DIAYN)** (Eysenbach et al., arXiv 1802.06070) maximizes I(S;Z) with a discriminator. It does produce varied gaits, but only when the state space and discriminator inputs make gross differences the easiest thing to tell apart. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06070)
- **Lipschitz-constrained Skill Discovery (LSD)** (Park et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2202.00914) states the failure mode directly: MI objectives "usually prefer static skills to dynamic ones." The I(Z;S) and Variational Intrinsic Control (VIC)-style objectives "can be fully maximized even with small differences in states as long as different z's correspond to even marginally different s_T's." As a result, "continuous DIAYN and DADS discover only posing skills on Ant" unless features are engineered. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00914) [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.00914)
- **Controllability-aware Skill Discovery (CSD)** (Park et al., ICML 2023) repeats the point: MI-based methods "often end up discovering 'static' skills with limited" state variation. [mlr](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/park23h/park23h.pdf)
- **Dynamics-Aware Discovery of Skills (DADS)** (Sharma et al., ICLR 2020, arXiv 1907.01657) shows the flip side. Adding predictability yields skills that are easy to model, which favours regular, repeatable motion. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.01657) That is close to your self-observer term.

**What this means for Hysteresis.** Your observer only needs some reversible mapping from the ~8 state dimensions into the 31 motion descriptors. The cheapest such mapping on this body is:
- arousal → speed or acceleration of a fixed gesture;
- beat-band phase → phase of the oscillation;
- band power → amplitude.

That is exactly the "same shape at different speeds/accelerations" you observed. Decoding reached ~0.94 because this mapping is close to lossless for an 8-D target. Nothing in the bound rewards spending visible posture on the information.

**Three features of your setup make it worse:**
1. **Within-song normalization erased song identity.** Z-scoring the target causally over 30 s removes each song's mean and variance at time scales above ~30 s. A sad ballad and a techno track end up with the same target distribution. Song-dependence in posture is therefore not just unrewarded but invisible to the objective. This is the single most important change, and it costs nothing.
2. **The target is low-dimensional and mostly fast.** Beat phase (cos/sin), arousal, surprisal and band powers mostly change at beat or sub-second scales. Timing modulation carries them well, and posture is the wrong channel for them.
3. **Costs are priced, but variety has no value.** Energy, jerk, hand-speed hinges and limit-proximity costs all grow with excursion. Any second shape that moves the shoulder or visits new regions of joint space has a cost and zero decoding benefit. The earlier "parked against end stops" failure, with zero gradient through the clip, was the same pressure seen through a bug.

**This is analogous to posterior collapse.** When a decoder can explain the data through a cheap channel, the expensive latent channel gets ignored. Here, timing is the cheap channel and posture is the expensive one. The standard remedies carry over directly:
- weaken the cheap channel (constrain the observer);
- raise the value of the expensive channel (targets that only posture can carry);
- or put a floor under the use of the expensive channel (diversity or coverage constraints).

### 1.3 Would decoding a richer target (16–32 PCs, song structure) fix it?

**Partly, and not on its own.** A 16–32-PC target raises the information that must pass through the motion. But LSD's argument still applies: if those PCs can be encoded by small differences in timing or amplitude, the optimizer will do that.

The richer target forces posture variety only when two conditions both hold:
- **(a)** some target dimensions are slow and differ between songs (section-level, song-level, slow-band layer power, without 30 s normalization), so they cannot be carried by beat-scale timing;
- **(b)** the observer cannot read small differences. That requires a Lipschitz bound, a perceptual noise floor, or a coarse posture-histogram input.

Expect a trade-off. Every skill-discovery paper that adds diversity pressure (LSD, DvD, Differentiable Quality Diversity (DQD)) reports some loss on the primary objective in exchange for coverage. Plan for decoding to fall from 0.94 to roughly 0.7–0.85 as the price of variety. This is an estimate, not a sourced figure.

## 2. What the perception and music-cognition evidence says "conveying the music" requires

### 2.1 What observers read from arm and robot motion

- **Pollick et al. (2001), "Perceiving affect from arm movement,"** *Cognition* 82: B51–B61.
  - Perceived affect from point-light arm movements formed a circumplex with an activation axis and a pleasantness axis. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010027701001470)
  - Activation correlated with "greater magnitudes of velocity, acceleration and jerk." [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010027701001470)
  - Pleasantness "appears to be carried in the phase relations between the different limb segments." [scispace](https://scispace.com/papers/perceiving-affect-from-arm-movement-4yb3pmqhkz)
  - **Implication:** speed modulation, which is what your arm does now, conveys only arousal. Valence and feeling need changes in inter-joint coordination: shoulder–elbow–wrist phase relations. Your policy holds these fixed (one shape, one neighbour-lag pattern).
- **Saerbeck & Bartneck (2010), HRI.** [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Perception-of-affect-elicited-by-robot-motion-Saerbeck-Bartneck/1b46471e6c4720727da2fd97ff6e882729c786ff) They varied acceleration and curvature on a Roomba and on iCat.
  - Acceleration predicted perceived arousal. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Perception-of-affect-elicited-by-robot-motion-Saerbeck-Bartneck/1b46471e6c4720727da2fd97ff6e882729c786ff) [springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-22698-9_16)
  - Valence was "at least partly encoded in an interaction between acceleration and curvature." [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Perception-of-affect-elicited-by-robot-motion-Saerbeck-Bartneck/1b46471e6c4720727da2fd97ff6e882729c786ff)
  - The type of embodiment had no effect. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Perception-of-affect-elicited-by-robot-motion-Saerbeck-Bartneck/1b46471e6c4720727da2fd97ff6e882729c786ff)
  - **Implication:** path curvature, i.e. the shape of the hand trajectory, has to vary, not just speed.
- **Bretan, Hoffman & Weinberg (2015),** *Int. J. Human-Computer Studies* 78:1–16 (Shimi, 5 DoF, faceless). Expressions were designed so that "posture is strongly correlated to valence and the velocities of the movements are correlated with arousal." [hrc2](https://hrc2.io/assets/pdfs/papers/BretanetalIJHCS15.pdf)
  - Moving gestures were recognized better than static postures: 81% vs 47% in person. [hrc2](https://hrc2.io/assets/pdfs/papers/BretanetalIJHCS15.pdf)
  - In-person recognition: happy and sad 100%, anger 91.7%; fear was at chance. [hrc2](https://hrc2.io/assets/pdfs/papers/BretanetalIJHCS15.pdf)
  - In a parameter sweep (n=10), posture height correlated with happy (r = .26) and sad (r = −.51). Volatility correlated with anger (.32) and sad (−.54). Exaggeration (range of motion) correlated positively with all six emotions (.11–.32). [hrc2](https://hrc2.io/assets/pdfs/papers/BretanetalIJHCS15.pdf)
  - **Caveat:** these studies ran without music. [hrc2](https://hrc2.io/assets/pdfs/papers/BretanetalIJHCS15.pdf)
  - **Implication for a 5-DoF arm:** overall posture height (shoulder elevation) is the valence channel, and range of motion raises perceived emotion across the board. Your shoulder sd of 3° shuts off both.
- **Knight & Simmons (2014), RO-MAN.** They turned the Laban Effort qualities (Time, Weight, Space, Flow) into motion features for low-DoF robots. Machine classification matched intended manner 41.7% of the time, against a 41.2% human benchmark. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Expressive-motion-with-x,-y-and-theta:-Laban-Effort-Knight-Simmons/d3f3d65606cbf93d95e391ff1fbf1ee1fdf450c5) [cmu](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/expressive-motion-with-x-y-and-theta-laban-effort-features-for-mobile-robots) Laban Effort features are therefore a usable, validated descriptor set for low-DoF bodies. The modest accuracies are a reminder that intended expression is often misread.

### 2.2 What humans do with their bodies to music

- **Burger et al. (2013),** *Frontiers in Psychology* 4:183. Sixty participants, 30 stimuli, optical motion capture, Bonferroni-corrected correlations. [frontiersin](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00183/pdf)
  - **Pulse clarity** correlated with whole-body measures: centre-of-mass speed r = .67, amount of movement .62, shoulder wiggle .67, hip wiggle .58, feet .55. "Clear pulses seemed to be embodied with the whole body." [frontiersin](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00183/pdf)
  - **Low-frequency flux (50–100 Hz, kick and bass)** correlated with head speed (r = .73). [frontiersin](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00183/pdf)
  - **High-frequency flux (6.4–12.8 kHz, hi-hats and cymbals)** correlated with hand speed (.65), hand distance (.57) and amount of movement (.57). [frontiersin](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00183/pdf)
  - **Percussiveness** correlated with upper-body and hand speed and with shoulder wiggle (.61). [frontiersin](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00183/pdf)
  - **Tempo** had no significant effect on any movement feature. [frontiersin](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00183/pdf)
- **Toiviainen, Luck & Thompson (2010),** *Music Perception* 28(1):59–70. Music-induced movement entrains to several metrical levels at once: [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Embodied-Meter:-Hierarchical-Eigenmodes-in-Movement-Toiviainen-Luck/f3090007166fdc8ea975962abaf5c75804b5e604)
  - mediolateral arm movements at the tactus;
  - torso rotation at 2 beats;
  - lateral flexion at 4 beats.
  The 2021 follow-up, "Embodied Meter Revisited" (*Music Perception* 39(3)), found vertical bouncing at 1 beat, hand anteroposterior movement at 2 beats and mediolateral sway at 4 beats. [ucpress](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/39/3/249/119731/Embodied-Meter-RevisitedEntrainment-Musical) The amplitude of these movement components depended on fluctuation in the music. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a1c9b9ad579b62991da9fde3ba4bb5f7e1e711)
- **Witek et al. (2014),** *PLoS ONE* 9(4):e94446. The desire to move and pleasure follow an inverted U against syncopation: medium syncopation is best. [plos](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0094446)

### 2.3 Mapping onto five joints (testable hypotheses, not established facts)

| Arm element | Human analogue | Should be driven by | Source basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base yaw + shoulder (large, slow) | Torso sway/rotation, centre-of-mass motion | Pulse clarity / beat-band confidence → amplitude. 2- and 4-beat periods. Slow-layer (0.01–0.5 Hz) state → mean posture height (valence-like) | Burger 2013; Toiviainen 2010/2021; Bretan 2015 |
| Elbow | Arm/hand distance | High-frequency flux, percussiveness → excursion | Burger 2013 |
| Wrist bend | Head nod | Low-frequency flux (kick/bass) → 1-beat bounce | Burger 2013 (head ↔ 50–100 Hz flux) |
| Wrist roll + mirror ball | Hands, fine ornament | High-band (8–20 Hz layer) surprisal → flicker. Ball reflections amplify small rolls | Inference from Burger; artistic |
| Inter-joint phase lag | Limb-segment phase relations | Valence-like slow state, harmonic or mode changes | Pollick 2001 |
| Stillness | — | Low arousal / low pulse clarity | Inference; Burger (less movement at low pulse clarity) |

These mappings are hypotheses for the learned system to discover and for evaluation to test. They should not be wired in. Use them as analysis contrasts, for example: "does shoulder amplitude correlate with pulse clarity across held-out songs, r > 0.3?" Wiring them in would break "nobody designed how it moves."

### 2.4 What robot-dance studies say about synchrony vs variety

- **Hoffman & Vanunu (2013), HRI** (Travis, n = 67, between-subjects).
  - 22/23 (96%) of participants in the off-beat condition said the robot moved on-beat. In that condition, beats were skipped or shifted by ±100–500 ms. [guyhoffman](http://guyhoffman.com/publications/HoffmanHRI13.pdf)
  - Off-beat and on-beat robots produced the same increase in "listening with me" ratings over a static robot (5.78 and 5.29 vs 3.32, F(2,64) = 18.19, p < .001). [guyhoffman](http://guyhoffman.com/publications/HoffmanHRI13.pdf)
  - The overall effect on song liking was not significant. [guyhoffman](http://guyhoffman.com/publications/HoffmanHRI13.pdf)
  - The robot used only "a constant repetitive motion according to the beat." [guyhoffman](http://guyhoffman.com/publications/HoffmanHRI13.pdf)
- **Avrunin, Hart, Douglas & Scassellati (Yale), "Effects related to synchrony and repertoire in perceptions of robot dance," HRI 2011, Lausanne, pp. 93–100.** The abstract states that "perceptions of the lifelikeness of the robot and the quality of the dance can be manipulated by simple changes, such as variation in the repertoire of motions, coordination of changes in behavior with events in the music, and the addition of flaws to the robot's synchrony with the music." Per search snippets (the full text was not reachable), the robot that was always on the beat was rated the best dancer, but the "half-on/half-off" robot was rated more lifelike.

**Implication:** beat lock alone does not make observers judge a robot musical, and observers barely detect it. Repertoire, and changes tied to musical events, matter more. That is exactly what your arm lacks. The Avrunin result also supports the "almost human, creepy" aim: imperfect synchrony reads as more alive.

## 3. Ranked architectural changes

Costs are estimates for your laptop plus Kaggle T4 setup, relative to your current 13–18 h full pass.

### Rank 1: Change what must be decoded (objective)

**Change:**
- **(a) Remove within-song z-scoring** for a subset of target dimensions. Normalize those with fixed statistics from the whole training corpus, so between-song differences survive.
- **(b) Add slow targets:** slow-layer (0.01–0.5 Hz) state, its top 8–16 principal components (PCs) fitted once on training songs, and section-boundary surprisal integrated over 8 s.
- **(c) Add a contrastive music↔motion term (InfoNCE).** Within a batch of B songs (B = 8–16), the observer embeds a 4–8 s motion window and the cortex state for the same window. It must pick the correct pairing out of B. Negatives from other songs at matched arousal make "song identity" impossible to encode through speed alone.

**Expected effect:** creates a real incentive for song-dependent behaviour. On its own it may still be satisfied by timing codes (see §1.3), so pair it with Rank 2.

**Cost:** low. The observer grows modestly, and contrastive batches can reuse rollouts.

**Risk:** the observer finds side channels, such as tempo, which already differs between songs. Mitigation: match negatives on tempo and RMS bins.

**Story fit:** strong and honest. The claim becomes "an observer can tell which music this body is listening to, from the body alone." That is itself a pre-registerable check, and it is the same test you run on humans.

### Rank 2: Make posture the only cheap channel (Lipschitz/perceptual observer)

**Change:**
- Constrain the observer to be 1-Lipschitz with respect to a posture metric: joint-space or hand/ball-position distance in metres, as in LSD. The mechanism is spectral normalization of observer layers over posture-space inputs. LSD implemented its constraint with spectral normalization (Miyato et al., 2018). [mlr](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/park23h/park23h.pdf)
- Alternatively, add observation noise of ~1–2 cm on hand position and ~3° on joints. This models finite visual acuity, so small differences become illegible.
- Feed the observer slow posture-occupancy features: soft histograms over a 3-D grid of mirror-ball positions, in 5 cm cells, over 4 s.

**Expected effect:** state differences can only be decoded if they produce metre-scale posture differences. LSD's reported result is exactly this ("more diverse, dynamic, and far-reaching skills"). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00914) The transfer from RL skill discovery to your gradient-through-physics setting is my inference; it has not been tested.

**Cost:** low.

**Risk:** pushes toward large excursions, which interacts with safety (Rank 4 handles that).

**Story fit:** good: "the audience has finite visual acuity; the body must make its states visible at a human scale."

### Rank 3: Explicit diversity pressure across songs (DvD-style determinant)

**Change:**
- For each batch of B songs, compute an embedding per song: mean and covariance of posture descriptors, or a soft posture histogram.
- Add −λ·log det K, where K is an RBF kernel matrix over the B embeddings.
- **Diversity via Determinants (DvD)** (Parker-Holder et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2002.00632) argues that determinants beat pairwise distances, because pairwise distance can "be high but diversity as measured by determinants is low" (clustering). [ox](https://oxford-man.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Effective-Diversity-in-Population-Based-Reinforcement-Learning.pdf) DvD also adapts the reward–diversity weight online with Thompson sampling. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.11114) [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.00632)
- In your case, set λ from a small grid at first, or use DvD's bandit.

**Expected effect:** directly penalizes identical shape statistics across songs. Because it is computed per song, it rewards song-dependence rather than random jitter.

**Cost:** low. B embeddings per batch, fully differentiable.

**Risk:** diversity unrelated to music, i.e. arbitrary per-song styles. That is why it must be paired with Rank 1. The contrastive term ties the diversity to the music.

**Alternative:** use quality-diversity (MAP-Elites) as an outer loop. Differentiable Quality Diversity (Fontaine & Nikolaidis, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2106.03894) introduced CMA-MEGA, which uses objective and measure gradients. It "significantly outperforms" derivative-free QD, including on an arm-repertoire domain. [liner](https://liner.com/review/differentiable-quality-diversity) It needs gradients of the measures, which you have through MJX, but it keeps an archive of many policies, which costs about 10–50× more rollouts. Use it only for the cheap toy stages (Stage 2), not full training.

### Rank 4: Turn penalties into constraints (cost shaping)

**Change:**
- Replace the quadratic energy and jerk costs with hinge constraints that are zero inside a safe envelope:
  - jerk below a measured threshold from the hand-tuned reference;
  - hand speed ≤ 1.0 m/s, set from servo and safety limits rather than taste;
  - floor margin ≥ 3 cm;
  - limit proximity ≥ 10°.
- Use Lagrange multipliers (dual ascent) so constraints are met without pricing interior motion.
- Add gravity compensation as a feedforward term in the controller, so holding the shoulder up costs nothing extra in the objective (it does cost torque on hardware, see §3.8).

**Expected effect:** variety is free inside the envelope. The policy no longer parks at the hinge (your 0.741 m/s pinning).

**Cost:** negligible.

**Risk:** multiplier oscillation. Use slow dual learning rates.

**Story fit:** "the body has limits, like a body; inside them it is free."

### Rank 5: Fix motor-program collapse, or remove the discrete layer

**Change:**
- Add a load-balancing auxiliary loss to the Gumbel-softmax selector. The Switch Transformer (Fedus et al., arXiv 2101.03961) uses α·N·Σ f_i·P_i. They "use an α = 10^-2," after sweeping 10^-1 to 10^-5, and found it "balanced load quickly without interfering with training loss." [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.03961)
- Compute the balancing over a song or a batch, not over time steps, so the arm may still hold one program within a passage.
- Add a per-song usage-entropy floor: at least 3 programs used for ≥10% of time across a song set.
- **Alternative:** drop the basal-ganglia (BG) discrete selector until Ranks 1–4 work. One continuous policy is easier to debug. Reintroduce BG later as "action selection" with an honest test (program switches cluster at musical boundaries).

**Expected effect:** prevents 100% use of a single program.

**Risk:** forced but meaningless switching. Check that switches align with surprisal peaks, as a pre-registered check.

### Rank 6: Controller/body changes that allow varied shapes

**Change:**
- Let the slow cortex layer drive centre (mean posture) and inter-joint phase lags directly through the motor-cortex modulation path. At present, the phase-oscillator parameterization makes "same shape, different frequency" the default.
- Add 2–3 learned synergy vectors. These are low-rank joint-coupling patterns, in the spirit of muscle synergies (Bizzi, d'Avella; not re-verified in this review), and they modulate amplitude per joint group.
- Keep the CPGs. Ijspeert's review, "Central pattern generators for locomotion control in animals and robots: a review" (*Neural Networks* 21(4):642–653, May 2008, doi:10.1016/j.neunet.2008.03.014), lists CPG properties including "allowing modulation of locomotion by simple control signals," which makes it a sound biological basis for descending modulation. The problem is not the CPGs but that only frequency, amplitude and κ were effectively used.

**Expected effect:** gives the optimizer a cheap path to new shapes. Pollick predicts that inter-joint phase relations are where valence lives.

**Cost:** low.

### Rank 7: A learned movement prior (optional, with licence caveats)

**Option:** pretrain a small motion VAE or diffusion prior on human dance, retarget it to 5 DoF (for example, human torso yaw → base, shoulder flexion → shoulder, elbow → elbow, wrist → wrist), and let the brain-state controller act in its latent space. The standard way to combine a style prior with a task objective is AMP (Peng, Ma, Abbeel, Levine & Kanazawa, "AMP: Adversarial Motion Priors for Stylized Physics-Based Character Control," ACM TOG 2021, doi:10.1145/3450626.3459670): "High-level task objectives… can be specified by relatively simple reward functions, while the low-level style… can be specified by a dataset of unstructured motion clips."

**What dance-generation work teaches:**
- **EDGE** (Tseng et al., CVPR 2023, arXiv 2211.10658) found its "beat alignment scores exceed those of ground truth samples." The authors argue that "dance is not strictly about matching local" beats. They rely on a large user study instead. [thecvf](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Tseng_EDGE_Editable_Dance_Generation_From_Music_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf) [liner](https://liner.com/review/edge-editable-dance-generation-from-music) [arxiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.10658) So do not adopt the beat-alignment score as a pass bar.
- **AIST++** (Li et al., arXiv 2101.08779): 5.2 h, 1408 sequences, 10 genres, 30 subjects, 60 pieces of music. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.08779v3)

**Honesty:** with a prior, "nobody designed how it moves" becomes "it learned its movement vocabulary from a human dancer and chose when to use it." That is truthful and arguably creepier ("almost human"), but the public wording must change.

**Rank rationale:** ranked below 1–6. It adds a data and licence dependency, and Ranks 1–4 may be enough. Try it only if Stage 3 fails the human test.

### Rank 8: Memory, retried in the one form that reliably gets used

**Why your four designs failed (consistent with the literature):**
- Learned retrieval has to earn its trust through credit assignment. When the parametric path already predicts well, the gradient to use retrieval is weak and noisy.
- Your oracle-replay result (it halved prediction error) shows the information is there. The trained gating never learned to rely on it.

**What works in practice is non-parametric retrieval with a fixed or validation-tuned blend:**
- **kNN-LM** (Khandelwal et al., ICLR 2020, arXiv 1911.00172) interpolates p = λ·p_kNN + (1−λ)·p_LM with λ "tuned on the validation set" and "no additional training." This gave a 2.9-point perplexity improvement (to 15.79). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00172)
- **Memorizing Transformers** (Wu et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2203.08913) use a non-differentiable kNN memory with a learned per-head scalar gate. "Most heads learned to attend almost exclusively to external memory." [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.08913) But their model is large and trained on long documents where memory is clearly useful.
- **Episodic control** uses fixed lookup rules rather than learned trust. Blundell et al. (DeepMind, including Hassabis), "Model-Free Episodic Control" (arXiv 1606.04460, June 2016), present it as "a simple model of hippocampal episodic control" and report that it reaches "a highly rewarding strategy significantly faster than state-of-the-art deep reinforcement learning algorithms." Pritzel et al.'s NEC (arXiv 1703.01988) is not re-verified here.

**Recommendation:**
- Do not retry a memory for prediction; the cortex does not need it.
- Retry it as motor memory. Keys are frozen cortex-state embeddings (slow and mid layers) at the current moment. Values are the motor parameters used last time a similar passage occurred.
- Output = (1−λ)·policy + λ·mean of the k nearest values, with λ fixed (0.2–0.3) and chosen on validation songs.
- Nothing is trained, so it cannot "learn not to use" itself.

**Story:** "when a passage returns, the body recalls how it moved before" (hippocampus-like, honest).

**Pre-registered check:** in songs with repeated choruses, the posture JS distance between chorus 1 and chorus 2 must be lower with memory than without (and than a time-shifted control).

**Cost:** trivial (FAISS or plain NumPy on ≤10^5 entries).

### 3.8 Sim-to-real (applies to every rank)

- **Torque class.** The SO-ARM100 repository states that "the 7.4V has a stall torque of 16.5kg.cm at 6V (and likely slightly less for a 5V power supply). The 12V version has a stall torque of 30kg.cm." Your 2.9 N·m figure is the 12 V part. [github](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100)
  - With the 7.4 V part (≈1.6 N·m), a shoulder holding a mirror ball at reach may run near stall. The drooping and folding you saw in sim will then be worse on hardware.
  - Specify the 12 V parts with a high reduction (1/345) for shoulder and elbow.
  - Keep continuous load below about 30–40% of stall. This is a hobby-servo rule of thumb, not sourced. Thermal derating on these servos is not documented in the primary sources I found.
- **Measured actuator parameters (third-party, unverified on your unit).** One public SO-101 project (so101-mjlab, GitHub) reports parameters fitted to STS3215 step responses: kp ≈ 18.3, kd ≈ 1.34, friction loss 0.20, armature 0.021, effort limit 1.47 N·m. It trained with 0–2 control-step delay randomization at 50 Hz. [github](https://github.com/vssinghh/so101-mjlab)
  - AutoSysID (GitHub) describes "a command-to-motion latency of tens of milliseconds" and uses DeepMind's mujoco.sysid. Its own status note says it is "not yet run on real hardware." [github](https://github.com/adimunot21/autosysid)
  - Treat both as starting points. Identify your own arm: chirp/step excitation per joint, fit kp/kv/friction/armature plus latency, then randomize ±30% around the fit.
- **MJX on CPU.** The official docs warn that MJX-JAX on a single scene "can be 10x slower than MuJoCo." [readthedocs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjx.html) Contact cost scales with possible, not active, contacts. MuJoCo Playground reports JIT compilation of 1–3 min. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08844)
  - Declare explicit contact pairs: ball↔table, ball↔upper arm, forearm↔table. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11048)
  - Use MJX only on the T4 for batched gradients. Use CPU MuJoCo for evaluation rollouts and human-study renders.
- **Your current proprioception delay** (10 ms) is probably optimistic for half-duplex serial reads of five servos plus command latency. Test 20–40 ms in sim.

## 4. Recommended redesigned architecture (end to end)

1. **Ear (keep):** 44 features at 100 Hz. Add pulse clarity (fluctuation-spectrum entropy, as in MIRToolbox) and sub-band flux at 50–100 Hz and 6.4–12.8 kHz as analysis covariates only. They are not motor inputs.
2. **Cortex B7 (keep, frozen during motor training):** four band-limited LRU layers, arousal and surprisal. Freezing it makes the monitor's "real internal state" stable and its pre-registered checks reusable.
3. **State interface (new):** two streams.
   - **Fast:** beat phase, arousal, surprisal; causal z-scoring over 30 s is fine here.
   - **Slow:** slow-layer PCs, 8 s surprisal integral and song-level band power, all with corpus-level normalization.
4. **Motor system:**
   - CPGs (keep) with κ beat coupling.
   - Motor cortex maps the fast stream to frequency, amplitude and phase. It maps the slow stream to posture centre, inter-joint lags and 2–3 synergy weights.
   - Gravity feedforward.
   - Basal ganglia: off in v1. In v2, 4–8 programs with Switch-style load balancing (α = 0.01), computed per song.
5. **Cerebellum (keep):** forward model from babbling. It also serves as a cheap surrogate for rollouts in toy stages.
6. **Hippocampus v5 (new, v2):** a frozen-key kNN motor memory with a fixed λ blend.
7. **Objective:**
   - Contrastive music↔motion InfoNCE with tempo/RMS-matched negatives;
   - plus a Lipschitz/noisy observer decoding fast and slow streams (keep the self-observer);
   - plus −λ·log det K song-diversity;
   - subject to hinge constraints with dual multipliers (speed ≤ 1.0 m/s, jerk envelope, 3 cm floor margin, 10° limit margin, torque ≤ 40% of stall);
   - minus a small switch cost.
8. **Real arm:** sysid-fitted actuators and latency randomization in sim. A safety layer on hardware duplicates the constraint envelope.

## 5. Staged experiment plan with pre-registered pass rules

Register each stage's rules (dated, hashed, in the repository) before running it. All metrics are computed on the 357 held-out songs unless stated otherwise.

**Stage 0: Measure the current policy (½ day, CPU).**
Compute the baseline metrics so later bars are relative to it:
- between-song JS distance of posture distributions (5 cm grid of ball position, 3-D);
- number of distinct postures (occupied cells at ≥0.5% dwell);
- shoulder sd per song;
- per-song peak hand speed;
- program-usage entropy;
- a machine 2AFC: a classifier picks which of two songs a 30 s motion clip came from.

Pre-registered expectation: the machine 2AFC is near chance (≤60%). If it is not, the diagnosis in §1 is partly wrong. Record that.

**Stage 1: Toy test of the collapse mechanism (1–2 days, CPU).**
Setup: a 2-link planar arm with synthetic "songs" (latent slow variable × fast beat). Train with:
- (i) the current 8-D within-song normalized decoding;
- (ii) plus corpus normalization and slow targets;
- (iii) plus a Lipschitz observer;
- (iv) plus DvD.

Pass: (iv) gives ≥3× the between-song JS distance of (i), with decoding ≥0.7. (iii) alone gives ≥1.5×. If (ii) alone suffices, simplify.

**Stage 2: Toy quality-diversity check (2–3 days, CPU or T4).** Run CMA-MEGA (pyribs) on the toy arm, with posture centroid and phase lag as measures. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03894) This tells you whether a diverse, high-legibility repertoire exists at all under your costs. Pass: ≥40% archive coverage at legibility within 0.1 of the best.

**Stage 3: Full 5-DoF training with Ranks 1–5 (one full pass, ~15–20 h; T4 for MJX).**
Pre-registered machine bars (all must pass):
- (a) Between-song posture JS distance (base-2, 0–1 scale): median across song pairs ≥ 0.3, and ≥ 50% of the hand-tuned planner's value.
- (b) Distinct postures ≥ 50% of the hand-tuned planner's count.
- (c) Shoulder sd ≥ 8° in ≥ 70% of songs.
- (d) Per-song peak hand speed varies: across-song sd ≥ 0.08 m/s, and fewer than 20% of songs within 2% of the speed bound.
- (e) Machine 2AFC song identification ≥ 75% on 30 s clips, with tempo-matched pairs.
- (f) Decoding beats the time-shifted and untrained controls (existing checks).
- (g) Embodied-cognition contrasts (directional, from §2.3): shoulder amplitude vs pulse clarity r > 0.2 across songs; wrist-bend speed vs low-band flux r > 0.2.
- (h) Constraint violations below 0.1% of time.

Any metric that is degenerate-passable must have a paired control. For example, JS distance must exceed the JS distance between a song and itself time-shifted by 60 s; otherwise random jitter passes.

**Stage 4: Human perceptual test (a weekend; ~€0–200 for online participants or friends).**
Design:
- 2AFC match/mismatch. Show a 20 s render of the arm with its own song vs the same clip dubbed with a different, tempo-matched song (±5 bpm), so beat alignment alone cannot decide.
- n ≥ 30 participants × 20 trials.
- Pre-registered pass: group accuracy ≥ 65%, and binomial test against 50% at p < .01.
- Second test: ratings of "conveys the feeling of the music" (7-point), learned policy vs hand-tuned planner vs a speed-only modulated single shape (your current policy as a control). Pass: learned > current policy, with the learned–planner difference d ≥ −0.2 (non-inferiority).
- Include an off-beat control in the spirit of Hoffman & Vanunu. If humans cannot tell on-beat from off-beat for your arm, do not claim beat-following publicly.

**Stage 5: Sim-to-real (1–2 weeks of evenings, hardware).**
Steps:
- sysid;
- replay 20 policy clips on hardware;
- measure tracking error (target: joint RMS < 4°);
- servo temperature over a 10-minute set;
- re-run the Stage-3 machine metrics on motion captured from video.

Pass: the metrics stay within 80% of their sim values.

**Stage 6 (v2): Motor memory and BG.** Pre-registered rules:
- Chorus-repeat posture similarity improves over no-memory and time-shifted controls (one-sided test, p < .05).
- Program switches occur within 1 s of surprisal peaks at ≥2× the rate under a circular-shift null.

## 6. What to drop

- **The embodied-prediction reward** ("move so moving helps predict"). The cortex does not need the body, as your own result showed, and RL exploited it. If you want this in the story, the honest framing is that the body expresses prediction; it does not serve it.
- **PPO for program selection.** It collapsed, and gradient-through-physics already works for you.
- **Learned retrieval keys, the learned recognition blend and uncertainty arbitration for the hippocampus.** Replace them with a frozen-key, fixed-λ motor memory.
- **Within-song 30 s z-scoring of the whole decoding target.** Keep it only for the fast stream.
- **Quadratic energy and jerk penalties as soft costs.** Make them hinge constraints.
- **Beat-alignment score, and decoding accuracy, as headline public claims.** EDGE shows the first can exceed real dancers, [liner](https://liner.com/review/edge-editable-dance-generation-from-music) and your run shows the second can be maxed by a degenerate gesture. Make human 2AFC plus song-dependence the headline.
- **Any thought of retargeting AIST++ into the exhibited work without AIST's written consent.**

## 7. Caveats and unverified items

- **Not tested anywhere:** the transfer of LSD's Lipschitz argument and DvD's determinant from RL skill discovery to gradient-through-differentiable-physics legibility training. The diagnosis is well supported; the size of the fix is my estimate.
- **Not re-verified in this review** (cited from domain knowledge, so check before quoting publicly): Bizzi/d'Avella synergies; Oudeyer's learning-progress work; Friston/Adams active inference; Dragan's legibility; LaViers; Huang Yi & KUKA; NEC; RETRO; InfoGAIL; Bailando and its FID_k/FID_g metrics; Phillips-Silver & Trainor. I did not pull specific claims from these into the recommendations. Ijspeert (2008), AMP (Peng et al., 2021) and Model-Free Episodic Control (Blundell et al., 2016) were checked against their bibliographic records and abstracts only.
- **Avrunin et al. (2011)** (HRI 2011, Lausanne, pp. 93–100): the abstract is confirmed. The on-beat vs half-on/half-off ratings come from search snippets, and the robot platform and sample size remain unconfirmed because the full text was not reachable. The Hoffman & Vanunu and Bretan et al. numbers come from the full papers.
- **The STS3215 actuator parameters** are from third-party GitHub projects. AutoSysID self-reports that it has not been run on hardware.
- **Numeric thresholds** in §5 (JS ≥ 0.3, 65% 2AFC, 8° shoulder sd, etc.) are proposed, not taken from literature. Calibrate them in Stage 0 against the hand-tuned planner and lock them before Stage 3.
- **Bretan et al.'s emotion recognition** was measured without music, and Burger et al.'s correlations are song-level averages over 30 stimuli. [hrc2](https://hrc2.io/assets/pdfs/papers/BretanetalIJHCS15.pdf) [frontiersin](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00183/pdf) Both are guides for analysis contrasts, not ground truth for a 5-DoF arm.
